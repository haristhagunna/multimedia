What Is Multimedia?

Unit 1 · topic 1 of 6

What Is Multimedia?Medium and multimedia

The word is built from multi (many, much, multiple) and medium.

  • A medium is the means for distribution and presentation of information.
  • Text, graphics, speech, music and video are all media.
  • Media can be classified by different criteria; the classification by perception (what the human senses: text, audio, video) is the one that suits the definition of multimedia.
  • The full six-way classification is in Classification of Media.
Source note: What Is Multimedia?

Medium and multimediaThree definitions to know (1/2)

Version Definition
Short A combination of text, graphics, sound, animation and video delivered interactively to the user by electronic or digitally manipulated means.
Field-based The field concerned with the computer-controlled integration of text, graphics, drawings, still and moving images (video), animation, audio and any other media, where every type of information can be represented, stored, transmitted and processed digitally.
Digital-information Digital information represented through audio, video and animation, in addition to traditional media such as text, graphics and images; an integration of these media, stored, transmitted and processed digitally.

All three agree on the same core ideas: integration of several media, digital representation, and (in the first definition) interactive delivery.

Source note: What Is Multimedia?

Medium and multimediaThree definitions to know (2/2)

Exam tip

A full-mark definition names all three ideas: integration of media, digital representation/processing, and interactivity. Writing only "many media together" earns partial marks.

The building blocks

Every multimedia application is assembled from five elements.

Text

Text and symbols are essential for communication. With the growth of the Internet and the WWW, text matters more than ever. The Web began as HTML, originally designed to display simple text documents on screens.

Source note: What Is Multimedia?

The building blocksAudio

Audio is produced by vibration and perceived by the sense of hearing. In multimedia it appears as speech, sound effects and music.

Graphics

A graphic is a two-dimensional figure or illustration. It may be produced manually (drawing, painting, carving) or by computer graphics technology. In multimedia, graphics show more clearly what the information is about: diagrams and pictures.

Video

Video is the technology of capturing, recording, processing, transmitting and reconstructing moving pictures. It consumes a lot of storage, so plan its use carefully.

Source note: What Is Multimedia?

The building blocksAnimation (1/2)

Animation is the rapid display of a sequence of images of 2D artwork or model positions to create an illusion of movement. It is an optical illusion caused by persistence of vision.

Extra

Persistence of vision means the eye keeps an image for a short moment after it disappears, so frames shown quickly one after another blend into smooth motion.

What Is Multimedia?History

Period Milestones
Early beginning Artist Bob Goldstein coined the term "multimedia" for his 1966 show Lightworks at L'Oursin.
1970s–1980s 1972: Atari's Pong, one of the earliest examples combining graphics and sound. 1976: Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak founded Apple. 1977: the Apple II, which used colour graphics. 1981: IBM PC. 1984: Apple Macintosh with a graphical user interface (GUI) and mouse.
1990s "Multimedia" was probably one of the most overused terms of the decade. The field sat at the crossroads of computing, telecommunications, publishing, consumer audio-video electronics, and television/movie/broadcasting.
2000s–present The Internet and streaming services made multimedia more accessible and interactive. Virtual reality, augmented reality and interactive media give more immersive experiences.

What Is Multimedia?Linear and non-linear multimedia

  • Linear content runs in a fixed order that the user cannot change, such as a book read page by page or a film.
  • Non-linear multimedia is interactive: the user has control over the content. Examples are games and interactive video.

Hypertext is text meant to be read non-linearly by following links. Hypermedia extends the links beyond text to graphics, images, sound and video. The World Wide Web is the best example.

A common diagram contrasts an open book labelled linear with a stack of pages joined by curved arrows through red "hot spots" labelled hypertext, non-linear.

Extra

The Multimedia System

Unit 1 · topic 2 of 6

The Multimedia SystemWhat is a multimedia system?

Two common wordings:

  • A multimedia system is a system capable of processing multimedia data and applications. It is characterised by the processing, storage, generation and manipulation of multimedia information.
  • A system that involves generation, representation, storage, transmission, search and retrieval of multimedia information. It also involves processing and analysis, including compression, and includes multimedia hardware, software and network.
Source note: The Multimedia System

The Multimedia SystemThe four basic characteristics

Exam tip

This is one of the most repeated topics: the four characteristics are always asked. Learn the four names and one supporting detail for each.

# Characteristic What it means
1 Computer controlled A computer controls every stage: producing content with image, sound and video editors; storing large shared information; transmitting it over a network; and presenting it through display, speakers or projector.
2 Integrated All components (text, audio, video, pictures, graphics, animation) are integrated. Devices such as the camera and microphone are connected to, and controlled by, a single computer. Digital storage is used for all media types, and the information is shown on the computer screen.
3 Digitally represented Analog signals must be converted to digital signals. Digital signals can be processed very effectively.
4 Interactive The final presentation is usually interactive: the user can select the time, order and speed of the presentation and can modify the content.
Source note: The Multimedia System

The Multimedia SystemChallenges of multimedia computing

  • Synchronization: maintaining the temporal relationship between different media types.
  • Sequencing: playing frames in the correct order and within the correct time frame.
  • Distributed networks: latency and delay over a network.
  • Storage and bandwidth: continuous media such as video need a lot of space to store and very high bandwidth to transmit.
  • Complex algorithms and hardware: multimedia spans many research areas and needs more complex and more efficient algorithms and hardware platforms.
  • Automatic analysis and indexing: analysing and indexing audio, image and video is much harder than for text.
Source note: The Multimedia System

The Multimedia SystemDesirable features of a multimedia system

Area Feature
Processing Very high processing power for large data and real-time delivery
Software A multimedia file system for audio and video streaming; software tools to handle and deliver media
Hardware Special hardware such as RAID; high, efficient I/O for real-time recording and playback
Compression Compression and decompression in real time
Capacity Storage in the order of terabytes, memory in the order of gigabytes, large caches and high-speed buses
Network Network support for client-server and distributed systems
Source note: The Multimedia System

The Multimedia SystemComponents of a multimedia system

Component Examples
Capture devices Video camera, video recorder, microphone, keyboards and mice, graphics tablets, 3D input devices, tactile sensors, VR devices, digitising hardware
Storage devices Hard disks, SSD, CD-ROM, DVD, Blu-ray
Communication networks Local networks, intranets, internet, Gigabit Ethernet, special high-speed networks
Computer system Multimedia desktop machines and workstations, processor, RAM, sound card, display card, MPEG/video/DSP hardware
Display devices High-resolution monitors, speakers, projector, HDTV, SVGA, colour printers

Capture, storage, communication, computing and display form a chain: media are captured, stored, sent, processed and shown.

Global Structure

Global StructureGlobal Structure

Exam tip

This is the one diagram you are most likely to be asked to draw and explain. Practise redrawing it from memory until you can place every box.

Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureThe big picture (1/3)

The global structure of multimedia is a framework that covers the devices, systems, applications and interactions needed to capture, process, store, transmit and present media in a cohesive and interactive way.

It has three domains and one cross-domain layer:

  1. Application domain (top)
  2. System domain (middle)
  3. Device domain (bottom)
  4. Synchronization, a vertical layer that runs through all three
Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureThe big picture (2/3)

In the usual diagram the three domains are stacked as boxes, with a tall vertical bar labelled Synchronization along the left side that spans all three.

Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureThe big picture (3/3)

Layer Boxes in the diagram
Application domain Tools and Applications (full-width bar); below it Documents, User Interface, Programming Abstractions
System domain Database Systems, Operating Systems, Communication Systems; below them a full-width bar, Computer Technology
Device domain Storage, Compression and Network; under Compression: Sound, Image/Graphics, Video/Animation
Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureApplication domain (1/2)

Provides the functions the user needs to develop and present multimedia projects. It includes software tools and a project-development methodology. The services of the system domain are offered to it through programming abstractions.

End-user software: media players, video editors, animation tools, presentation software.

Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureApplication domain (2/2)

  • Documents. Multimedia-enhanced documents that combine text, images, audio and video: e-learning materials, interactive PDFs, web pages. Document handling means generating the different media and recording them at presentation time.
  • User interface. Lets people interact with the system: buttons, menus, touch interfaces. It must be intuitive, responsive and multimedia-friendly.
  • Programming abstractions. APIs, libraries and frameworks (media APIs, graphics libraries, game engines) that spare developers low-level complexity.
Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureSystem domain (1/2)

Includes all the support for using the functions of the device domain: operating systems, communication systems (networking) and database systems. Computer technology is the layer that connects the device domain to the system domain.

Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureSystem domain (2/2)

  • Database systems. Store and manage multimedia objects (audio, video, images) with support for searching, indexing and retrieval. Example: multimedia databases for video libraries and digital archives. They also allow structured access to large databases.
  • Operating systems. Manage the resources multimedia needs: CPU, memory, disk and I/O devices. They provide real-time scheduling and device drivers (sound card, graphics card). Examples: Windows, Linux, macOS.
  • Communication systems. Handle data exchange between systems with protocols and data synchronization. Examples: TCP/IP and RTP (for real-time media). They are responsible for data transmission.
Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureDevice domain (1/2)

Covers the basic concepts and skills for processing the media elements and handling physical devices: cameras, microphones, speakers and other input/output hardware. It includes the processing of digital signals, image, graphics, animation, audio and video.

Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureDevice domain (2/2)

  • Compression. Raw multimedia data is very large. Compression reduces its size for faster transmission and efficient storage. Examples: MP3, AAC (audio), JPEG, PNG (image), MPEG, H.264 (video). It is essential for streaming, video conferencing and multimedia applications.
  • Network. Carries multimedia data between systems for streaming, online video calls and distributed applications. It requires high bandwidth and low latency.
  • Storage. Holds content permanently or temporarily: hard disks, SSDs, cloud storage. It must support large data volumes and fast read/write.
Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureThe synchronization layer (cross domain)

A vertical layer that affects all levels of the system. Synchronization is the coordination of multiple media types in time.

  • Audio must match video during playback.
  • Subtitles must appear at the correct moments.
  • It is critical for video conferencing, movies and e-learning.
  • Poor synchronization causes audio-video mismatch and a poor user experience.

Some sources call this layer the cross domain: it handles the temporal relationships among media across all domains. Unit 10 is devoted to it.

Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureWhere students go wrong

Network appears twice

Network (the physical carrier, needing bandwidth and low latency) is in the device domain. Communication systems (protocols such as TCP/IP and RTP) are in the system domain. Do not put compression, storage or network in the system domain.

Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureCheckpoint (1/3)

Check yourself

Name the three domains from top to bottom and one component of each.

Application (e.g. user interface), System (e.g. operating systems), Device (e.g. compression). The vertical synchronization layer runs across all of them.

Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureCheckpoint (2/3)

Check yourself

Why does synchronization sit across all domains rather than in one?

Because timing between media must be preserved at every level: when devices capture and play, when the OS schedules, when the network delivers and when the application presents. A mismatch at any level breaks the experience (for example, lips out of sync with audio).

Source note: Global Structure

Global StructureCheckpoint (3/3)

Check yourself

In which domain does the operating system belong, and what two services does it give multimedia?

The system domain. It provides real-time scheduling and device drivers (for example for the sound or graphics card), while managing CPU, memory, disk and I/O.

Classification of MediaClassification of Media

Exam tip

This is the most examinable block of the unit. Expect a direct question such as "Classify media" or "Explain the types of media with examples".

A medium is the means for distribution and presentation of information. Media can be classified by different criteria. These notes use six categories, each defined by a central question.

Remember it by asking the question

If you cannot decide which category something belongs to, ask each central question in turn. The one that fits is the answer.

Source note: Classification of Media

Classification of MediaThe six categories at a glance

Medium Central question Examples
Perception How do humans perceive information in a computer environment? Seeing: text, image, video. Hearing: music, noise, speech.
Representation How is the information coded in the computer? ASCII, JPEG, PCM, PAL/NTSC/SECAM, MPEG
Presentation Through which devices is information delivered to and introduced into the computer? Output: paper, screen, speaker. Input: keyboard, mouse, camera, microphone.
Storage How will information be stored? Hard disk, CD-ROM, SSD, floppy disk, microfilm
Transmission Over what will information be transmitted? Coaxial cable, fibre optics, free air space (wireless)
Information exchange Which carrier is used to exchange information between places? Storage plus transmission combined, e.g. e-mail
Source note: Classification of Media

Classification of MediaPerception medium

Perception media help humans sense their environment. The answer to the central question is: through seeing and hearing.

  • Seeing: text, image and video.
  • Hearing: music, noise and speech.
Source note: Classification of Media

Classification of MediaRepresentation medium

Defined by the internal computer representation of information: the formats used to code each kind of media.

  • Text is coded in ASCII .
  • Graphics are coded according to the CEPT or CAPTAIN videotext standards.
  • Images can be stored in JPEG format.
  • Audio streams are coded with PCM .
  • Audio-video sequences can be coded in different TV standards (PAL, NTSC, SECAM) and stored in the computer in MPEG format.
Source note: Classification of Media

Classification of MediaPresentation medium

The tools and devices for the input and output of information.

  • Output media: paper, screen, speaker.
  • Input media: keyboard, mouse, camera, microphone.

Storage medium

The data carrier that enables storage of information: hard disk, CD-ROM and similar. Microfilm, floppy disk and SSD also count.

Transmission medium

The different information carriers that enable continuous data transmission: coaxial cable, fibre optics, and free air space, which is used for wireless traffic.

Source note: Classification of Media

Classification of MediaInformation exchange medium

Includes all information carriers for transmission: that is, all storage and transmission media. Information can be exchanged by transporting a storage medium to the destination, or through computer networks such as an electronic mailing system.

Watch out

Some summaries list the information exchange medium as "VR, Cloud, Internet". The full definition is storage plus transmission media, for example e-mail. Use the full definition in the exam.

Source note: Classification of Media

Classification of MediaCommon confusions

Mistake Correct reading
"A monitor is a perception medium." A monitor is a presentation medium. Perception is the human sense (eyes, ears).
"E-mail is a transmission medium." E-mail is an information exchange medium: it combines storage and transmission.
"JPEG is a storage medium." JPEG is a representation format. The disk that holds the file is the storage medium.
"A cable stores data." A cable is a transmission medium; it only carries data.
Source note: Classification of Media

Media Concepts and Properties

Media Concepts and PropertiesMedia Concepts and Properties

This page covers the part of syllabus item 1.2 that deals with properties and notation. The syllabus never uses the word notation; the closest material is Media Concepts, the three things every medium defines.

Media concepts

Each medium defines:

  1. Representation space
  2. Representation values
  3. Representation dimensions

Representation space

The surrounding where the media are presented.

  • Visual representation space: paper, screen.
  • Acoustic representation space: mono, stereo.
Source note: Media Concepts and Properties

Media conceptsRepresentation values

Determine how the information is represented.

  • Continuous values: for example electromagnetic waves.
  • Discrete values: for example text characters in digital form.
Source note: Media Concepts and Properties

Media conceptsRepresentation dimensions (1/2)

A representation space has a spatial dimension and a temporal dimension.

  • Spatial dimension: two-dimensional (a monitor) or three-dimensional (holography).
  • Temporal dimension:
    • Time-independent media are discrete media. The information is a sequence of individual elements with no time component. Example: a document.
    • Time-dependent media are continuous media. Information is expressed not only by its individual values but also by its time of occurrence. Example: a movie.
Discrete media Continuous media
Time component None (time-independent) Essential (time-dependent)
Information is A sequence of individual elements Values and the time at which they occur
Examples Text, still images, a document Audio, video, a movie
Source note: Media Concepts and Properties

Media conceptsRepresentation dimensions (2/2)

Same words, two meanings

These notes use "continuous" and "discrete" for representation values (a continuous wave versus a digital character) and again for media types by time dependence. In an exam, say which one you mean. The "text plus pictures is not multimedia" argument below uses the time-dependence meaning.

Properties of a multimedia system

"Multimedia" is not any arbitrary combination of media. A multimedia system has three main properties.

Source note: Media Concepts and Properties

Properties of a multimedia system1. Combination of media

  • A simple text-processing program that incorporates images is often called a multimedia application, because two media are handled by one program.
  • But one should speak of multimedia only when both continuous and discrete media are utilised.
  • So a text processor with pictures is not a multimedia application.
Source note: Media Concepts and Properties

Properties of a multimedia system2. Independence

An important aspect of different media is their level of independence from each other. In general independence of different media is requested, but multimedia may require several levels of independence.

  • Tightly coupled: a computer-controlled video recorder stores audio and video together. There is an inherent, tight connection between the two media, coupled through a common storage medium.
  • Independent: for presentation, the combination of DAT (digital audio tape recorder) signals and computer-available text satisfies the request for media independence.
Source note: Media Concepts and Properties

Properties of a multimedia system3. Computer-supported integration

The computer is an ideal tool for media combination. The system should be capable of computer-controlled media processing, and should be programmable by a system programmer or even by the user.

Properties versus characteristics

Do not mix this page up with The Multimedia System.

Three properties (this page) Four characteristics
Names Combination of media, independence, computer-supported integration Computer controlled, integrated, digitally represented, interactive
Focus What makes a combination of media a multimedia system What a multimedia system is like in operation
Source note: Media Concepts and Properties

Media Concepts and PropertiesCheckpoint (1/3)

Check yourself

Is a word processor with embedded photographs a multimedia application? Why or why not?

No. Text and photographs are both discrete (time-independent) media. Multimedia needs both discrete and continuous media, so adding an audio clip or video would make it a multimedia application.

Source note: Media Concepts and Properties

Media Concepts and PropertiesCheckpoint (2/3)

Check yourself

Explain discrete versus continuous media with one example each.

Discrete media have no time component and are a sequence of individual elements (a text document). Continuous media are time-dependent: information is expressed by its values and by when they occur (a movie).

Source note: Media Concepts and Properties

Media Concepts and PropertiesCheckpoint (3/3)

Check yourself

What does 'independence' mean for media in a multimedia system?

The degree to which media can be handled separately from one another. A video recorder stores audio and video tightly coupled on a common storage medium, whereas DAT audio signals combined with computer text for presentation are independent.

Data Streams and Transmission ModesData Streams and Transmission Modes

This page covers syllabus item 1.3, data stream characteristics for traditional and continuous media.

Serial transmission

Multimedia communication is generally transmitted serially: one bit at a time. The three modes below differ in how the receiver stays in step with the sender.

Source note: Data Streams and Transmission Modes

Data Streams and Transmission ModesAsynchronous transmission

  • Synchronization at the byte level.
  • There may be gaps between bytes, which makes it good for random communication.
  • Examples: WWW, e-mail, keyboard.
Extra

Because there is no shared clock, each byte is wrapped with a start bit and a stop bit so the receiver knows where it begins and ends. That is why the textbook notes that synchronous transmission has no start and stop bits.

Source note: Data Streams and Transmission Modes

Data Streams and Transmission ModesSynchronous transmission

  • No start and stop bits.
  • Sender and receiver must be synchronized, using clocks.

Isochronous transmission

  • Data is transferred at a fixed rate.
  • It delivers data at a steady rate.
  • It is best for multimedia communication.
Source note: Data Streams and Transmission Modes

Data Streams and Transmission ModesComparison

Asynchronous Synchronous Isochronous
Timing unit Byte level Clock shared by sender and receiver Fixed, steady rate
Gaps allowed? Yes, between bytes Clock-driven Data arrives at a steady rate
Start/stop bits Used (implied) None Not specified
Best for Random, bursty traffic Clock-aligned transfer Multimedia
Examples WWW, e-mail, keyboard – Audio and video streams
Source note: Data Streams and Transmission Modes

Data Streams and Transmission ModesWhy multimedia needs a steady rate

Continuous media only make sense if they arrive on time and in order: a late audio sample is a click, a late video frame is a stutter. That is why it is called isochronous transfer "best for multimedia", and why the challenges of latency, delay and synchronization keep returning (see The Multimedia System).

Do not confuse these

Synchronization (the cross-domain layer that coordinates media in time) is not the same as synchronous transmission (a transmission mode where sender and receiver share a clock). Name each one clearly in your answers.

Source note: Data Streams and Transmission Modes

Data Streams and Transmission ModesCheckpoint (1/3)

Check yourself

Which transmission mode is best for multimedia, and why?

Isochronous, because it transfers data at a fixed, steady rate, which is what continuous media such as audio and video need.

Source note: Data Streams and Transmission Modes

Data Streams and Transmission ModesCheckpoint (2/3)

Check yourself

Give two differences between asynchronous and synchronous transmission.

Asynchronous synchronizes at the byte level and allows gaps between bytes (e.g. keyboard, e-mail, WWW). Synchronous uses no start and stop bits and needs sender and receiver to be synchronized with clocks.

Source note: Data Streams and Transmission Modes

Data Streams and Transmission ModesCheckpoint (3/3)

Check yourself

Is the synchronization layer in the global structure the same as synchronous transmission?

No. The synchronization layer coordinates different media in time across all domains (for example audio with video). Synchronous transmission is a mode of sending data in which sender and receiver share a clock.

  • B. Representation medium

Quick check 3 / 612. Which of these is a continuous (time-dependent) medium?

  • A. A text document
  • B. A still photograph
  • C. A movie
  • D. A page of ASCII characters
Answer: C. A movie's information depends on its time of occurrence. The others have no time component.

Quick check 4 / 613. Which transmission mode is best for multimedia?

  • A. Asynchronous
  • B. Synchronous
  • C. Isochronous
  • D. Parallel
Answer: C. Isochronous transfer is at a fixed, steady rate. Asynchronous allows gaps between bytes; synchronous removes start and stop bits and relies on shared clocks; parallel is not one of the three modes, and multimedia communication is generally serial.

  • A. Application domain
  • B. System domain
  • C. Device domain
  • D. Synchronization layer
Answer: C. The system domain holds operating, database and communication systems; the application domain holds tools, documents, user interface and programming abstractions.
