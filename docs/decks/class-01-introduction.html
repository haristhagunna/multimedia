About the courseCourse objective

Official objective

To introduce the technologies, concepts and techniques associated with the development of multimedia systems.

By the end you should be able to

  • Explain how sound, images and video are represented in a computer.
  • Calculate storage, data rate, compression ratio and schedulability.
  • Describe how multimedia is stored, scheduled, transmitted and synchronized.
  • Apply the ideas in practical work: audio, images, compression and scheduling.

Why multimedia mattersYou already use it every day

Streaming and calls

Video and audio must be compressed, sent in real time and kept in sync.

Games and animation

Graphics, sound and interaction are all scheduled against strict deadlines.

Learning and libraries

E-learning, digital libraries and archives combine text, images, audio and video.

Broadcast and storage

Digital TV, CDs, DVDs and Blu-ray rely on the standards you will study.

Think

Pick one app on your phone. Which of its media must be compressed, and which must arrive on time?

Cold-call two students for their answer to the Think box.

Why multimedia mattersWhat is multimedia?

Discrete media

Do not depend on time.

  • Text
  • Still images
  • Graphics

Continuous media

Depend on time; late data is useless.

  • Audio
  • Video
  • Animation
Exam focus

A multimedia system is computer-controlled, integrated, handles digital media and is interactive, with at least one continuous medium.

This definition returns in Unit 1. Ask students to remember the four words: computer-controlled, integrated, digital, interactive.

Reading order

First learn what media are (units 1–4), then how to shrink and store them (5–6), how a system delivers them on time (7–10), and finally how to build and use them (11–12).

Trace the path of a video call: capture, compress, schedule, send, synchronize, play.

SyllabusFour parts, one story

Part 1 · Foundations · Units 1–4

What multimedia is; how sound, images, video and animation are represented, stored in files and transmitted.

Part 2 · Compression and storage · Units 5–6

Run-length, Huffman, predictive and adaptive coding; JPEG, H.261, MPEG, DVI; CD, DVD and Blu-ray media.

Part 3 · Systems · Units 7–10

Real-time operating systems and scheduling (EDF, rate monotonic); communication and QoS; hypertext, SGML, ODA, MHEG; synchronization.

Part 4 · Building and applying · Units 11–12

Abstraction levels, libraries, toolkits and object-oriented approaches; video on demand, conferencing, education and information systems.

Each part builds on the one before. Compression needs the representation from Part 1; scheduling needs the data rates from Part 2.

Definition + diagram + example

Short answers: state the idea, draw it, give one concrete example.

Formula, substitution, result with units. Method marks are given even if the final value is wrong.

04

Laboratory work

Two practical hours every week.

Laboratory workPractical topics in the syllabus

  1. Integration of multimedia: audio, speech and music, video, static media, movie and animation programming.
  2. Image enhancement in Photoshop and Flash.
  3. 2D and 3D animation in OpenGL, Maya, Flash or C++.
  4. Image compression algorithm: JPEG.
  5. Real-time scheduling algorithm.
Bring

A notebook for lab records, headphones, and any photos or short audio clips you want to work on.

Laboratory workLabs and tools on this site

Python labs

Sampling and aliasing, Huffman and RLE, DCT and quantization, EDF and RM scheduling, synchronization drift. Every script is tested.

Tool labs

GIMP for images and colour, Audacity for audio, FFmpeg for conversion and compression.

Simulators

Six live simulators in the browser: sampling, Huffman, scheduler, bucket, DCT and a calculator.

Lab record

Write aim, steps, results with screenshots, and a short reflection for each lab.
