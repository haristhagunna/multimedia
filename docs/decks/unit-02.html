What is sound?

  • Physical phenomenon produced by vibration of matter
  • Air-pressure variations travel as a wave
  • Waveform repeats at regular intervals: one period
  • Periodic: instruments, vowels · Non-periodic: coughs, "s", "t", "f"

Sampling and quantization

  • Nyquist: sampling rate ≥ 2 × highest frequency
  • CD: 44,100 Hz → up to 22,050 Hz
  • Quantization: 3-bit = 8, 8-bit = 256, 16-bit = 65,536 levels
  • Fewer bits → lower quality

Quality versus file size

S = R × (b / 8) × C × D

R rate (samples/s) · b bits · C channels (1 mono, 2 stereo) · D seconds

10 s stereo, 44.1 kHz, 16-bit = 44100 × 2 × 2 × 10 = 1,764,000 bytes ≈ 1.68 MB

MIDI

  • Musical Instrument Digital Interface (early 1980s)
  • Stores instructions, not samples
  • Two parts: hardware (port, cable) + data format
  • Message: start of note + intensity
  • 16 channels · 128 instruments (0 piano, 12 marimba, 40 violin, 73 flute)

Speech: basic notions

  • Phone: smallest speech sound unit
  • Allophones: variants of a phone
  • Morph: smallest unit that carries meaning
  • Voiced: vocal cords oscillate (m, v, l)
  • Unvoiced: vocal cords open (f, s)
