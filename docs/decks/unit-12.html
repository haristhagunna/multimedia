Application Classes

Unit 12 · topic 1 of 4

Application ClassesIn plain words

The idea

Applications can be grouped by what they do: games, repositories, interactive TV and VoD, conferencing and mail, shared workspaces and business workflow.

Real-life picture

A game console, a digital library, a video call and a company document system are four different classes.

Remember it

Six classes: games, repositories, iTV/VoD, conferencing/mail, shared workspaces, business workflow.

Application ClassesApplication Classes (1/3)

Class Description
Game systems The leaders in using multimedia technology because the market is very large and demands on quality, although intense, are not crucial to the success
Multimedia repositories Mostly play-back-only systems. End users do not usually add information; input and output of the workflow are independent. Like games, but the database is much larger and the data needs indexing
Interactive TV and video on demand Usually developed from cable TV technology. The set-top box connects the digital network to the TV and other home appliances (telephone, fax). Besides the cable converter function it offers a full interactive multimedia interface to services; standardising the interfaces (set-top box to network and to home appliance) is critical
Video/phone conferencing and hypermedia mail Seeing the other person is a major improvement over voice only. More functions: interactive whiteboard, sharing paper-based diagrams, sharing computer output. Video messages are kept longer than voice messages, so need much more storage
Shared workspaces and executive environments Shared workspace: a user runs applications and the output appears on screens at remote locations. Shared executive environment: users at different places run the same application on their own workstation with the same data
Business process workflow applications Depend on the business process being served. Traditional relational databases must be extended to handle multimedia elements; object-oriented databases are a more natural medium for multimedia objects

System Types and Components

Unit 12 · topic 2 of 4

System Types and ComponentsIn plain words

The idea

Multimedia systems come in several types and are built from input, output and storage components.

Real-life picture

A multimedia PC: webcam and microphone (input), screen and speakers (output), SSD and DVD (storage).

Remember it

Three component groups: input, output, storage.

System Types and ComponentsTypes of multimedia systems

Type Description
Home / entertainment systems Mostly interactive but not live; the interaction is completely pre-programmed. May include a PC and a set-top box plus a TV, connected to a cable service or an Internet service
Business systems: dedicated Creation, storage and manipulation of multimedia objects happen completely within the system
Business systems: departmental Use a LAN for shared object storage management and processing; support a specific business process shared by most users in the department
Business systems: enterprise-wide Many LANs and WANs interconnected; share departmental or enterprise-level storage and processing; support local, departmental and inter-departmental applications

Development Cycle

Unit 12 · topic 3 of 4

Development CycleIn plain words

The idea

Making a multimedia product follows five steps: plan and cost, design, develop and produce, test and debug, deliver.

Real-life picture

Making an educational app: plan the budget, storyboard screens, build, test with students, publish.

Remember it

Plan → Design → Produce → Test → Deliver, and always check copyright.

Development CycleThe cycle at a glance

Development CycleDevelopment Cycle

Source note: Development Cycle

Development Cycle1. Planning and costing (1/2)

Main concerns:

  • capture the ideas and requirements of you or your clients;
  • identify the potential audience and users;
  • find the benefit of developing the application;
  • evaluate feasibility and cost of the whole project (production, testing, delivery).

A "back-of-the-envelope" or "paper napkin" approach is often used: capture ideas and judge feasibility quickly. The most important considerations:

Source note: Development Cycle

Development Cycle1. Planning and costing (2/2)

Factor Points
Hardware The most common limiting factor for development time and end users: poor or no sound device, limited storage, very narrow network bandwidth
Software Development software is expensive; software needed by end users adds up
Contents Existing material may not match needs, may be copyrighted (permission may be refused); new material is expensive and slow
Skills Very broad: computer, artistic and application-domain skills

It helps to build a pilot project or prototype first.

Source note: Development Cycle

Development Cycle2. Designing

A creative activity needing computer skills, talent in graphic arts, video and music, and knowledge of the subject area.

Storyboarding

A storyboard describes the project in exact detail with words and sketches for each screen image, sound and navigational choice. It can be very detailed (every screen down to colour, shade, text and attributes) or only a schematic guide, drawn on paper or with a computer tool.

Source note: Development Cycle

2. DesigningArchitecture

The arrangement of the multimedia information; a well-organised document lets users find information efficiently; start early. Types:

Architecture Structure
Linear One path from start to end
Hierarchical Tree of menus and sub-menus
Non-linear Free links between nodes (hypermedia)
Composite A mix of the above
Source note: Development Cycle

2. DesigningUser interface (1/3)

  • The emphasis is on multimedia presentation; content selection is the key and is limited by the size and complexity of the presentation, the quality of information, display hardware limits, and the need for completeness and coherence.
  • Media must be "adequate": to teach tennis, graphics and video suit better than text.
  • Coordination means composing the media.

Interface techniques (the remote camera surveillance example: a camera server on a serial interface, a remote client sends commands, video is digitised, compressed and sent):

Source note: Development Cycle

2. DesigningUser interface (2/3)

  • Keyboard: fixed commands on keys.
  • Buttons in a GUI: clicking "left" pans left.
  • Scroll bars beside the video window.
  • Special device: a joystick is more natural.
  • Direct manipulation of the video window: click a point to centre the camera there.

User-friendliness is the primary goal, though what it means and how to measure it are not always clear:

Source note: Development Cycle

2. DesigningUser interface (3/3)

  • Easy to learn and easy to remember instructions (for occasional and daily users);
  • Effective instructions: logically connected functions presented together and similarly; graphical symbols beat text; media exchangeable between applications;
  • Prompt feedback after each user action;
  • Usable by professional and occasional users.

3. Developing and producing

Production is when the project is actually rendered. Tasks: acquire all media elements, compose them according to the storyboard, track media elements and track progress, and have a way (or an expert) to solve technical problems quickly.

Source note: Development Cycle

3. Developing and producingRights and permissions

  • You must know who owns acquired content.
  • There are nine types of protected works: literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works; sound recordings, cinematograph films, television broadcasts, sound broadcasts, published editions of works.
  • License the rights before use; you may negotiate outright ownership or a licence.
  • Questions to settle: how the material will be used and distributed; fixed period?; exclusive or non-exclusive?; where distributed?; does the owner have authority to assign rights?; remuneration?
Source note: Development Cycle

Development Cycle4. Testing and debugging

An important and time-consuming phase.

Alpha testing Beta testing
Who Internal, the in-house team A wider range of testers, representative of real users, not those who built it

The product is used by many users who know little about computers and on many platforms and configurations, so test widely.

Source note: Development Cycle

Development Cycle5. Delivering

  • Plan delivery early.
  • CD-ROM and the Internet are the two most popular means (today also app stores and streaming).
  • Plan installation and use.
  • The distribution must include all media elements (clips), runtime libraries (Director runtime), drivers (DirectX), helper programs (QuickTime viewer, Acrobat reader) and the installation program and compression/decompression programs.
Exam tip

The five stages are a certain question. Add storyboard, architectures and alpha/beta testing for full marks.

Application Areas in Detail

Unit 12 · topic 4 of 4

Application Areas in DetailIn plain words

The idea

Multimedia is used in video on demand, video conferencing, education and training, information systems and entertainment.

Real-life picture

Online classes, movie streaming and digital museum guides.

Remember it

VoD needs a server, a network and a set-top box. Conferencing needs low delay.

Application Areas in DetailApplication Areas in Detail

Extra

The course material covers application classes, system types and components and the development cycle only. This page is written from standard textbook knowledge to cover the rest of syllabus 12.1–12.4. Check details against your prescribed textbook.

Source note: Application Areas in Detail

Application Areas in Detail12.1 Video on demand (VoD) (1/2)

Video on demand lets a user select and watch a video at any time, with VCR-like controls (play, pause, fast forward, rewind).

Source note: Application Areas in Detail

Application Areas in Detail12.1 Video on demand (VoD) (2/2)

Type Meaning
True VoD Each user gets an individual stream, started instantly, full interactivity
Near VoD The same film is started on several channels at staggered times (for example every 15 min); users wait for the next start; much cheaper

Requirements: large server storage and bandwidth (MPEG-1 about 1.5 Mbit/s, MPEG-2 about 4 to 6 Mbit/s per stream), real-time disk scheduling, admission control and QoS guarantees, billing and security.

Server capacity

A server with 1 Gbit/s output can serve about 1,000 ÷ 4 = 250 MPEG-2 streams at 4 Mbit/s each.

Source note: Application Areas in Detail

Application Areas in Detail12.2 Video conferencing

Video conferencing carries audio and video (plus shared tools) between two or more sites in real time.

  • Point to point and multipoint (a multipoint control unit, MCU, mixes or switches streams).
  • Desktop and room-based systems; features: shared whiteboard, application sharing, file transfer, chat.
  • Requirements: low delay (about 150 ms or less), small jitter, lip synchronisation (±80 ms), bandwidth adaptation, echo cancellation.
  • Standards: H.320 (ISDN), H.323 (packet networks), H.261 video coding (Unit 5), and SIP with RTP today.
Source note: Application Areas in Detail

Application Areas in Detail12.3 Educational and industrial applications

  • Educational: computer-based training (CBT), e-learning and virtual classrooms, simulations and virtual laboratories, educational games, multimedia encyclopedias, interactive tutorials with self-assessment.
  • Benefits: self-paced, rich explanation with animation and video, immediate feedback.
  • Limits: development cost and the need for good instructional design.

Industrial: employee training and safety simulations, product demonstration and catalogues, kiosks, CAD/CAM visualisation, process monitoring with video and sensor data, remote maintenance with video and annotation, corporate presentations.

Source note: Application Areas in Detail

Application Areas in Detail12.4 Information systems, archives, entertainment, editors, trends (1/2)

Information systems (kiosks): public touch-screen terminals giving maps, timetables, tourist and government information; need simple navigation and robust hardware.

  • Multimedia archives and digital libraries: collections of text, images, audio and video stored digitally with metadata.
  • Needs: digitisation and compression, indexing (text, content-based retrieval of images and video), search, long-term preservation, access control and copyright, distributed access over networks.
  • Examples: national archives, museum collections, film and music archives.

Media entertainment: computer games, interactive and digital TV, music and video streaming, special effects in films, theme-park rides.

Source note: Application Areas in Detail

Application Areas in Detail12.4 Information systems, archives, entertainment, editors, trends (2/2)

Media editors: tools to create and modify media: image editors (Photoshop), audio editors, non-linear video editors (cut, effects, transitions on a timeline), animation tools (Flash, Maya), authoring tools that integrate media.

Trends: streaming and mobile multimedia, high-definition and 4K video with efficient codecs, virtual, augmented and mixed reality, cloud-based services, AI-assisted creation, search and captioning, 5G low-latency networks, social and user-generated media.

Exam tip

For "short notes", give a definition, a block diagram or a list of requirements, and two examples. For VoD always name true vs near VoD.
