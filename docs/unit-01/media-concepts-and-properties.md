# Media Concepts and Properties

This page covers the part of syllabus item 1.2 that deals with **properties and notation**. The syllabus never uses the word *notation*; the closest material is **Media Concepts**, the three things every medium defines.

## Media concepts

Each medium defines:

1. **Representation space**
2. **Representation values**
3. **Representation dimensions**

### Representation space

The **surrounding where the media are presented**.

- **Visual** representation space: paper, screen.
- **Acoustic** representation space: mono, stereo.

### Representation values

Determine how the information is represented.

- **Continuous** values: for example electromagnetic waves.
- **Discrete** values: for example text characters in digital form.

### Representation dimensions

A representation space has a **spatial** dimension and a **temporal** dimension.

- **Spatial dimension:** **two-dimensional** (a monitor) or **three-dimensional** (holography).
- **Temporal dimension:**
    - **Time-independent** media are **discrete media**. The information is a sequence of individual elements with **no time component**. Example: a document.
    - **Time-dependent** media are **continuous media**. Information is expressed not only by its individual values but also by its **time of occurrence**. Example: a movie.

| | Discrete media | Continuous media |
|---|---|---|
| Time component | None (time-independent) | Essential (time-dependent) |
| Information is | A sequence of individual elements | Values **and** the time at which they occur |
| Examples | Text, still images, a document | Audio, video, a movie |

!!! exam "Same words, two meanings"
    These notes use "continuous" and "discrete" for **representation values** (a continuous wave versus a digital character) and again for **media types** by time dependence. In an exam, say which one you mean. The "text plus pictures is not multimedia" argument below uses the **time-dependence** meaning.

## Properties of a multimedia system

"Multimedia" is **not any arbitrary combination of media**. A multimedia system has three main properties.

### 1. Combination of media

A simple text-processing program that incorporates images is often called a multimedia application, because two media are handled by one program. But one should speak of multimedia **only when both continuous and discrete media are utilised**. So a text processor with pictures is **not** a multimedia application.

### 2. Independence

An important aspect of different media is their **level of independence** from each other. In general independence of different media is requested, but multimedia may require **several levels** of independence.

- **Tightly coupled:** a computer-controlled **video recorder** stores audio and video together. There is an inherent, tight connection between the two media, coupled through a **common storage medium**.
- **Independent:** for presentation, the combination of **DAT** (digital audio tape recorder) signals and computer-available text satisfies the request for media independence.

### 3. Computer-supported integration

The computer is an **ideal tool for media combination**. The system should be capable of **computer-controlled media processing**, and should be **programmable** by a system programmer or even by the user.

## Properties versus characteristics

Do not mix this page up with [The Multimedia System](multimedia-system.md).

| | Three **properties** (this page) | Four **characteristics** |
|---|---|---|
| Names | Combination of media, independence, computer-supported integration | Computer controlled, integrated, digitally represented, interactive |
| Focus | What makes a combination of media a multimedia system | What a multimedia system is like in operation |

## Checkpoint

??? question "Is a word processor with embedded photographs a multimedia application? Why or why not?"
    No. Text and photographs are both **discrete** (time-independent) media. Multimedia needs **both discrete and continuous** media, so adding an audio clip or video would make it a multimedia application.

??? question "Explain discrete versus continuous media with one example each."
    **Discrete** media have no time component and are a sequence of individual elements (a text document). **Continuous** media are time-dependent: information is expressed by its values and by when they occur (a movie).

??? question "What does 'independence' mean for media in a multimedia system?"
    The degree to which media can be handled separately from one another. A video recorder stores audio and video tightly coupled on a common storage medium, whereas DAT audio signals combined with computer text for presentation are independent.
