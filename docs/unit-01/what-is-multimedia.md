## Medium and multimedia

The word is built from **multi** (many, much, multiple) and **medium**.

A **medium** is the **means for distribution and presentation of information**. Text, graphics, speech, music and video are all media. Media can be classified by different criteria; the classification by **perception** (what the human senses: text, audio, video) is the one that suits the definition of multimedia. The full six-way classification is in [Classification of Media](media-classification.md).

### Three definitions to know

| From | Definition |
|---|---|
| Unit 1, p. 2 | A combination of **text, graphics, sound, animation and video** delivered **interactively** to the user by electronic or digitally manipulated means. |
| Unit 1, p. 3 | The field concerned with the **computer-controlled integration** of text, graphics, drawings, still and moving images (video), animation, audio and any other media, where every type of information can be **represented, stored, transmitted and processed digitally**. |
| *mul c1*, p. 5 | **Digital information** represented through audio, video and animation, in addition to traditional media such as text, graphics and images; an **integration** of these media, stored, transmitted and processed digitally. |

All three agree on the same core ideas: **integration of several media**, **digital** representation, and (in the first definition) **interactive** delivery.

!!! exam "Exam tip"
    A full-mark definition names all three ideas: *integration of media*, *digital representation/processing*, and *interactivity*. Writing only "many media together" earns partial marks.

## The building blocks

Every multimedia application is assembled from five elements.

### Text

Text and symbols are essential for communication. With the growth of the Internet and the WWW, text matters more than ever. The Web began as **HTML**, originally designed to display simple text documents on screens.

### Audio

Audio is produced by **vibration** and perceived by the sense of hearing. In multimedia it appears as **speech**, **sound effects** and **music**.

### Graphics

A graphic is a **two-dimensional figure or illustration**. It may be produced manually (drawing, painting, carving) or by **computer graphics** technology. In multimedia, graphics show more clearly what the information is about: diagrams and pictures.

### Video

**Video** is the technology of **capturing, recording, processing, transmitting and reconstructing moving pictures**. It consumes a lot of storage, so plan its use carefully.

### Animation

**Animation** is the **rapid display of a sequence of images** of 2D artwork or model positions to create an illusion of movement. It is an optical illusion caused by **persistence of vision**.

!!! info "Beyond the lecturer's notes"
    Persistence of vision means the eye keeps an image for a short moment after it disappears, so frames shown quickly one after another blend into smooth motion.

| Element | What it is | Key idea to remember |
|---|---|---|
| Text | Symbols and characters | HTML began as simple text display |
| Audio | Vibration heard by the ear | Speech, effects, music |
| Graphics | 2D figures and illustrations | Drawn by hand or by computer |
| Video | Moving pictures captured and reconstructed | Needs large storage |
| Animation | Rapid sequence of images | Persistence of vision |

## History

| Period | Milestones |
|---|---|
| **Early beginning** | Artist **Bob Goldstein** coined the term "multimedia" for his 1966 show *Lightworks at L'Oursin*. |
| **1970s–1980s** | 1972: Atari's **Pong**, one of the earliest examples combining graphics and sound. 1976: Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak founded Apple. 1977: the **Apple II**, which used colour graphics. 1981: **IBM PC**. 1984: Apple **Macintosh** with a **graphical user interface (GUI) and mouse**. |
| **1990s** | "Multimedia" was probably one of the most overused terms of the decade. The field sat at the crossroads of computing, telecommunications, publishing, consumer audio-video electronics, and television/movie/broadcasting. |
| **2000s–present** | The Internet and **streaming services** made multimedia more accessible and interactive. Virtual reality, augmented reality and interactive media give more immersive experiences. |

!!! exam "Exam tip"
    The lecturer calls the Apple II "the first computer to use color graphics". Safer wording in your own answer: "an early, popular computer with colour graphics".

## Aspects of multimedia

| Aspect | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Interactivity** | Interaction between users and content: clicking, dragging, entering text. |
| **Integration** | Different media types combined into one cohesive application. |
| **Hyperlinking** | Pieces of content connected by links, as on websites. |
| **User control** | The user controls pace and flow: pause, play, skip. |
| **Multisensory experience** | Engages several senses: sight, hearing, touch. |

## Linear and non-linear multimedia

- **Linear** content runs in a fixed order that the user cannot change, such as a book read page by page or a film.
- **Non-linear** multimedia is **interactive**: the user has **control over the content**. Examples are **games** and **interactive video**.

**Hypertext** is text meant to be read **non-linearly by following links**. **Hypermedia** extends the links beyond text to graphics, images, sound and video. The **World Wide Web** is the best example.

## Checkpoint

??? question "Why is 'a combination of many media' not a complete definition of multimedia?"
    Because the lecturer's definitions also require that the media be **integrated under computer control**, **represented digitally**, and (usually) delivered **interactively**. A slideshow of printed photos beside a radio is many media, but not multimedia.

??? question "Which element depends on persistence of vision?"
    **Animation**: a rapid sequence of still images that the eye blends into apparent motion.

??? question "Give one example each of linear and non-linear presentation."
    Linear: a film or a printed book. Non-linear: a video game or an interactive video, where the user's choices change what happens next.
