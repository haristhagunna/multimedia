# Basic Sound Concepts

## What is sound?

**Sound is the physical phenomenon produced by the vibration of matter.**

When matter vibrates, **pressure variations** are created in the surrounding air. This alternation of **high and low pressure** travels through the air in a **wave-like motion**. When the wave reaches the human ear, a sound is heard.

## Waveform and period

- The pattern of the oscillation is called a **waveform**.
- The waveform repeats the same shape at regular intervals. One such interval is called a **period**.
- Natural sound waves are **never perfectly smooth or uniformly periodic**.

A typical diagram shows air pressure plotted against time as a smooth wave: the vertical distance from the middle line to a peak is the **amplitude**, and the horizontal distance of one full repeat is **one period**.

### Periodic and non-periodic sound

| | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Periodic** sound | Musical instruments, vowel sounds, whistling wind, bird songs |
| **Non-periodic** sound | Unpitched percussion instruments, coughs, sneezes, rushing water, consonants such as "t", "f" and "s" |

## Frequency

The **frequency** of a sound is the **reciprocal of the period**. It is the **number of periods in one second**, measured in **hertz (Hz)** or cycles per second (cps).

$$\text{frequency} = \frac{1}{\text{period}} \qquad 1\ \text{kHz} = 1000\ \text{Hz}$$

### Frequency ranges

| Range | Frequencies |
|---|---|
| **Infrasound** | 0 – 20 Hz |
| **Human audible sound** | 20 Hz – 20 kHz |
| **Ultrasound** | 20 kHz – 1 GHz |
| **Hypersound** | 1 GHz – 10 THz |

Human audible sound is also called **audio** or **acoustic signals (waves)**. **Speech** is an acoustic signal produced by humans.

!!! exam "Exam tip"
    Learn the four ranges in order, with the numbers. The audible range (**20 Hz to 20 kHz**) is the one used in every later calculation, including the Nyquist theorem.

## Amplitude

A sound has a property called **amplitude**, which humans perceive subjectively as **loudness or volume**. The amplitude is the **measure of the displacement of the air pressure wave from its mean (quiescent or idle) value**. The greater the amplitude, the louder the sound.

Loudness is measured in **decibels (dB)**:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 dB | Essentially no sound heard |
| 35 dB | Quiet home |
| 70 dB | Noisy street |
| 120 dB | Discomfort |

| Property | Physical meaning | What we perceive |
|---|---|---|
| **Frequency** | Periods per second (Hz) | Pitch |
| **Amplitude** | Displacement from the mean pressure | Loudness (volume) |

!!! info "Extra"
    Amplitude is linked amplitude to loudness. The usual pairing for frequency is **pitch** (higher frequency, higher note); pitch is mentioned only later, in the speech section.

## Sound versus audio

| | **Sound** | **Audio** |
|---|---|---|
| Form of energy | **Mechanical wave energy** (longitudinal sound waves) | **Electrical energy** (analog or digital signals) that represents sound |
| What it is | Vibrations through a material; the **action** | The **end result**: the technology to hear sounds from natural or human-made sources |
| Nature | A **continuous wave** travelling through air, measured as the pressure level at a point | A signal made from that wave |

A **microphone** is the bridge: the sound field moves its surface according to the varying pressure, and a **transducer** converts this energy into a voltage level (electrical energy).

!!! exam "Exam tip"
    "Differentiate sound and audio" is a classic short question. Write both rows: **mechanical wave energy versus electrical signal**, and **action versus end result**.

## Checkpoint

??? question "Define frequency and give the audible range."
    Frequency is the **reciprocal of the period**: the number of periods per second, measured in hertz. The human audible range is **20 Hz to 20 kHz**.

??? question "What does amplitude determine, and how is it measured?"
    Amplitude is the displacement of the air pressure wave from its mean value. Humans perceive it as **loudness** and it is measured in **decibels**.

??? question "Is a vowel sound periodic or non-periodic? And a consonant such as 's'?"
    A vowel sound is **periodic**. Consonants such as "t", "f" and "s" are **non-periodic**.
